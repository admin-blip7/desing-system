# Design Identity Archetypes

This document explains the design identities currently built into the app and the target outcome when creating a brand.

Source of truth in code: `src/lib/data/personalities.ts`

## Why Archetypes Exist

Most teams struggle to define style direction with enough specificity to be reusable.

Archetypes solve that by providing structured identity DNA:

- typography system
- color behavior
- layout logic
- motion behavior
- photography direction
- voice and tone rules

Instead of starting from a blank page, teams start from a coherent strategic design pattern and adapt.

## The Brand-Creation Goal

When a user creates a brand in this app, the goal is to produce a system that is:

- clear enough for non-design teams to execute,
- flexible enough for multiple channels,
- strict enough to protect consistency,
- expressive enough to remain differentiated.

## Identity DNA Model

Every identity profile can define:

- `archetype`: strategic design posture
- `philosophy`: guiding creative intent
- `ideal` / `notIdeal`: where the style works or fails
- `dna.typography`: families, structure, reading behavior
- `dna.colors`: palette strategy and usage rules
- `dna.layout`: spacing density and hierarchy style
- `dna.motion`: animation principles and timing behavior
- `dna.photography`: image style and emotional tone
- `dna.voice`: language and messaging style

## Available Identity Profiles

## 1. Apple

- Archetype: premium reduction and clarity
- Signature: minimal surface, high legibility, controlled emphasis
- Best fit: premium tech, high-trust categories

## 2. Teenage Engineering

- Archetype: playful technical precision
- Signature: industrial labels, modular visual logic, strong object identity
- Best fit: hardware, creative tools, maker products

## 3. Polestar

- Archetype: Scandinavian restraint
- Signature: neutral typography, quiet layouts, controlled contrast
- Best fit: premium automotive, architecture, high-end consulting

## 4. Linear

- Archetype: high-velocity SaaS clarity
- Signature: dense-but-readable product UI language
- Best fit: software, developer tools, operational platforms

## 5. Bang & Olufsen

- Archetype: material luxury
- Signature: editorial spacing, tactile-material narrative
- Best fit: luxury audio, hospitality, premium real estate

## 6. Nothing

- Archetype: transparent retro-futurism
- Signature: stark contrast + signal accents + exposed-system aesthetics
- Best fit: disruptive tech and youth-forward hardware brands

## 7. Aesop

- Archetype: intellectual apothecary
- Signature: warm editorial language with ritual-oriented voice
- Best fit: wellness, beauty, boutique experiential brands

## 8. Manual Mode

- Archetype: custom from zero
- Signature: no template constraints, full user-defined direction
- Best fit: teams with strong existing vision or mixed references

## How Archetypes Drive the Product

Archetypes influence:

- onboarding interpretation
- prompt construction for AI generation
- design token defaults
- workspace expectations
- export tone and structure

This means identity is not cosmetic metadata. It is an operational input to the whole system.

## What Contributors Can Improve

- add new archetypes with complete DNA dimensions
- improve existing archetype realism and applicability
- refine ideal/non-ideal mapping for better recommendation quality
- align archetype output with module-level acceptance criteria
- validate identity consistency in generated exports

## Quality Bar for New Identity Profiles

A profile is ready when it includes:

- clear strategic intent,
- practical visual rules (not vague adjectives),
- real usage constraints,
- cross-channel applicability,
- compatibility with existing generation and workspace flows.
